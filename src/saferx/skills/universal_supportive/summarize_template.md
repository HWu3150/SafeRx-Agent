Focus the summary on factors that drive universal supportive medication needs:

1. **ICU/critical care context**: Is the patient in ICU, intubated, on vasopressors, post-surgical, or otherwise critically ill? These drive prophylactic needs.
2. **Immobility / VTE risk**: Bed-bound, post-operative, trauma, or prolonged hospitalization → VTE prophylaxis.
3. **GI stress / aspiration risk**: NPO status, intubation, critical illness, steroid use → stress ulcer prophylaxis (PPI/H2-blockers), bowel management (laxatives, stool softeners), antiemetics.
4. **Pain / sedation needs**: Surgical procedures, trauma, burns, invasive lines → analgesics, sedatives.
5. **Electrolyte context**: Renal function, diuretic use, critical illness → electrolyte monitoring and repletion (Ca, K, Mg).
6. **Nutritional status**: NPO, TPN, tube feeding, prolonged admission → vitamins, IV fluids, dextrose.
7. **Infection / vaccine status**: Any hospitalization → influenza vaccine consideration; critical illness → infection prophylaxis.
8. **Prior medications**: Note any supportive medications from prior visits that suggest continuation.

Do NOT summarize specialty-specific clinical details (cardiac rhythm, tumor staging, etc.) — those belong to the routed specialty skills.
