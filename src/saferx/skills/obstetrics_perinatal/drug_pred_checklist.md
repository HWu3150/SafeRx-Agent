Medication classes are most plausible when supported by:
- obstetric analgesia, antiemetic, acid suppression, bowel support, and VTE prophylaxis in peri-delivery context
- antihypertensive or seizure-prevention context when hypertensive disorders are explicit
- uterotonic, antibiotic, or postpartum-supportive classes only when procedure or complication evidence is present
- neonatal/perinatal-specific support only when the record is actually about the neonate/perinatal condition

Be careful:
- do not insert routine maternity meds without delivery or complication context
- distinguish maternal medications from fetal/neonatal diagnoses

## How to apply
- Predict a class when at least one listed trigger is documented for the current admission, or it is an ongoing prior-visit medication.
- Not enough: a diagnosis name without admission-level context, remote history, or the care setting alone.
- Any **block** → do not predict.
- Within a group the first class is the default; a class marked *second-line* needs its stated condition. One indication → one class unless a combination is noted.

## Uterotonics
Uterotonics are often stopped before medication reconciliation: predict them only when the agent or its use is explicitly documented, not from delivery alone.
- **H01BB** oxytocin and analogues (oxytocin, carbetocin) — oxytocin/Pitocin named for third-stage management, induction/augmentation, or postpartum hemorrhage/atony. **Block:** antepartum without induction; fetal monitoring only.
- **G02AB** ergot alkaloids (methylergonovine), *second-line* — uterine atony or PPH not responding to oxytocin. **Block:** hypertensive disorder of pregnancy; routine delivery with adequate oxytocin response.

## Hypertensive disorders
- **C02DB** hydralazine — gestational hypertension, preeclampsia or eclampsia; severe-range BP (SBP ≥160 or DBP ≥110); acute postpartum hypertension. **Block:** normotensive; HTN without severe-range BP or OB diagnosis.

## Peripartum analgesia and local care
- **M01AB** ketorolac/diclofenac — post-cesarean pain; post-delivery cramping; perineal laceration pain in addition to acetaminophen. **Block:** active labor or antepartum (premature ductus closure); renal impairment; NSAID allergy.
- **D04AB** topical anesthetics (lidocaine gel, benzocaine, pramoxine) — perineal laceration or episiotomy repair; postpartum perineal pain; postpartum hemorrhoids; topical lidocaine named; airway topical spray. Epidural/spinal anesthesia is N01BB. **Block:** cesarean under spinal alone; delivery without laceration; antepartum without procedure.
- **R02AX** throat preparations (lozenges, sprays) — sore throat after intubation for general anesthesia; post-intubation airway discomfort; documented throat symptoms. Chlorhexidine oral care is a different group. **Block:** labor or vaginal delivery without intubation.

## Genital infections
- **G01AF** vaginal imidazoles (miconazole, clotrimazole) — vulvovaginal candidiasis in a pregnant or postpartum patient (first-line because oral azoles are teratogenic); yeast-infection symptoms with documented treatment; recurrent VVC; candidiasis risk (gestational diabetes, antibiotics) with symptoms. Oral fluconazole is J02AC; oral thrush is A07AA. **Block:** bacterial vaginosis (that is G01AA); systemic fungal infection; no documented fungal infection.
- **G01AA** vaginal antibiotics (clindamycin) — bacterial vaginosis in pregnancy; GBS prophylaxis when penicillin allergy precludes the IV regimen; vaginal infection needing topical antibiotic. **Block:** GBS screening without positive culture or plan; systemic antibiotics only; candidiasis (that is G01AF).

## Pregnancy maintenance
- **L02AB** progestogens — preterm-birth prevention (prior preterm birth, short cervix; vaginal progesterone or 17-OHP caproate); threatened preterm labor with progesterone; postpartum abnormal uterine bleeding. **Block:** routine pregnancy without preterm risk; contraception planning; postmenopausal hormone therapy.
